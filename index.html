01 · Pharmacology

How does it work?

Sitagliptin selectively inhibits dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4), slowing the inactivation of incretin hormones.

Mechanism summary: incretin activity is glucose-dependent; sitagliptin is not a substitute for insulin in type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis.

Class

DPP-4 inhibitor

Oral incretin-based glucose-lowering medicine.

Effect

Glucose-dependent

Enhances insulin secretion and suppresses glucagon when glucose is elevated.

Practical point

Renal dose adjustment

Assess eGFR before treatment and periodically; reduce dose in moderate/severe renal impairment.

02 · Label vs real-world use

Indication & usage map

Separate regulatory approval from clinical research and unapproved use. Approval varies by jurisdiction and product label.

Approved / on-label (US label)

Type 2 diabetes mellitus in adults

As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control. May be used alone or with selected other glucose-lowering agents as described in the product label.

Not indicated for type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis. A history of pancreatitis requires clinical consideration because this population was not adequately studied in the label.

Clinical context

Combination therapy

Can be prescribed with agents such as metformin, sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones or insulin where supported by the applicable label and individual clinical circumstances.

Combination use within T2DM is not automatically an off-label indication; check the local product information.

03 · Safety intelligence

Adverse reactions & precautions

Clinical-trial frequency and post-marketing reports answer different questions. Do not mix them into one frequency ranking.

Commonly reported in trials / label

The US label identifies upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis and headache among reactions reported in ≥5% and more often than placebo. Hypoglycaemia is more common when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea.

Important post-marketing safety topics

  • Acute pancreatitis: reported; seek urgent assessment for persistent severe abdominal pain.
  • Hypersensitivity: anaphylaxis, angioedema and severe skin reactions have been reported.
  • Severe joint pain: a class warning for DPP-4 inhibitors.
  • Bullous pemphigoid: rare post-marketing reports; blistering skin lesions need evaluation.
  • Renal function: acute renal failure has been reported; dose adjustment is required with reduced eGFR.

Post-marketing reports cannot by themselves establish incidence or causality.

04 · Evidence triangulation

What does the evidence actually say?

Triangulate regulatory labeling, randomized controlled trials, systematic safety analyses and spontaneous reports—without treating them as equivalent.

TECOS · randomized outcomes trial
14,671

Participants with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease were randomized to sitagliptin or placebo added to usual care.

Primary cardiovascular outcome: 11.4% vs 11.6%; HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.88–1.09).

Interpretation: cardiovascular safety non-inferiority was shown; this does not mean sitagliptin prevents cardiovascular events.

Pancreatic safety analysis
0.3% vs 0.2%

Pancreatitis occurred in 23 sitagliptin-assigned participants and 12 placebo-assigned participants in the cited TECOS analysis.

HR 1.93 (95% CI 0.96–3.88); P=0.065.

Interpretation: event numbers were small and the confidence interval was wide; continued vigilance is appropriate.

Regulatory label

High authority for approved use

Best source for labeled indication, contraindications, dose and warnings.

Randomized trials

Strong for studied outcomes

Best for comparative efficacy and prespecified safety outcomes in enrolled populations.

Spontaneous reports

Signal detection, not incidence

Useful for detecting potential signals; affected by under-reporting, stimulated reporting and confounding.

06 · Project insights

Five takeaways

01

One clear approved role

The best-established indication is glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise.

02

Safety is context-dependent

Hypoglycaemia risk is particularly relevant when sitagliptin is combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

03

Kidney function matters

Renal assessment and label-based dose adjustment are central to safe use.

04

Signal ≠ proof

Post-marketing reports are valuable for signal detection but cannot estimate true risk alone.

05

Do not overstate off-label use

Research interest in other conditions does not equal an approved or guideline-recommended indication.

06

Cardiovascular profile

TECOS supports cardiovascular safety neutrality in its studied population, not cardiovascular risk reduction.
